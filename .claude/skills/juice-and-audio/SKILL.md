# Juice and Audio

## Purpose

Make game interactions feel responsive and emotionally legible in mobile ads. Juice must strengthen the mechanic and creative idea; it must not hide inaccurate gameplay or create visual noise.

## Inputs

- Approved creative brief
- Visual direction
- Camera plan
- Verified gameplay sequence
- Target placement and duration
- Emotional territory

## Animation principles

Use only the motion needed to communicate or reward:

- Anticipation
- Clear input response
- Ease-in and ease-out
- Follow-through
- Overshoot
- Squash and stretch when stylistically appropriate
- Impact timing
- Progress timing
- Reward timing
- Controlled looping

Animation should preserve the apparent game mechanic and timing.

## Particles and visual effects

Use effects for:

- Input confirmation
- Direction
- Success
- Failure
- Progress
- Reward
- Impact
- Transformation

Possible effects include sparks, dust, trails, glow, bursts, shards, smoke, dissolve, confetti, pulses, and screen-edge feedback. Control density, color, size, and duration so the core action remains readable.

## Transitions

Choose transitions that support the concept:

- Match cut
- Shape transformation
- Wipe
- Zoom transition
- Morph
- Whip movement
- Impact transition
- Clean gameplay-to-end-card transition

Do not use a transition only because it looks impressive. It should communicate continuity, change, escalation, or payoff.

## Sound design

Define:

- Input and tap sounds
- Swipe and movement sounds
- Collision and error sounds
- Success and reward sounds
- Whooshes and impacts
- UI feedback
- Background music
- Tension escalation
- ASMR or tactile sounds
- Silence and pauses

Audio should match the emotional territory. Calm ads need space and restraint; challenge ads may use escalating rhythm and sharper feedback.

## Feedback hierarchy

Prioritize:

1. Input confirmation
2. Gameplay consequence
3. Major progress or failure
4. Reward or payoff
5. Supporting ambience

The strongest cue should belong to the most important action.

## Review rules

- Does every major effect support attention, understanding, emotion, or reward?
- Is the interaction still readable without relying on effects?
- Are motion and sound consistent with the visual direction?
- Are effects too dense for mobile?
- Does the sound match the visible action?
- Does the timing preserve truthful gameplay?

## Boundaries

This skill does not choose the campaign strategy, define the visual identity, choose aspect ratios, or rewrite the game mechanic.
