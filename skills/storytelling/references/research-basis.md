# Research basis for game-hook storytelling

Researched 2026-10-30. These are attributed source recommendations and project interpretations, not universal formulas or new measured project learnings. Local owner requirements remain authoritative. Original web content is linked rather than copied.

## Sources read and their useful contribution

### 1. Game-specific light narrative

[Giacomo Maragliulo, Supersonic creative director, writing in PocketGamer.biz, 2025-09-11](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/why-stories-sell-the-emotional-trigger-for-mobile-game-players/)

The practitioner proposes a light narrative with a starting situation, a trigger, progression and an ending. He connects narrative to gameplay and player motivations, and discusses different structures and viewpoints. Project interpretation: use this to diagnose missing causality or payoff, not to require four separate shots. The article also discusses 15–30-second storytelling; adapting to 4–5 seconds requires selecting a smaller unit. Its broad performance assertions are practitioner claims without the underlying campaign dataset on the page; they are not a performance guarantee.

### 2. Connect the opening to the game's appeal

[TikTok for Business, Creative Tips: Hyper-Casual Games, 2023-06-14](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/tiktok-hyper-casual-game-creative-tips/pc/en)

TikTok explicitly describes its patterns as observations rather than mandatory rules. It distinguishes direct game attractions for core audiences from relatable/comic openings used to reach broader audiences, and stresses a smooth transition into gameplay or reasons to play. Project interpretation: broad emotional hooks can be legitimate, but articulate the bridge; do not mistake attention for game relevance. This older, platform/category-specific guidance does not establish our audience, campaign results, or an exact universal timing threshold.

### 3. Attention and connection are separate concerns

[Google Ads Help, About the ABCDs of effective video ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551?hl=en)

Google distinguishes attention, branding, connection and direction. It recommends reaching the heart of the story quickly, keeping the message focused, and using humor, surprise or intrigue. Project interpretation: check whether an opening both draws interest and serves its intended product story. Guidance for whole YouTube ads should not become a requirement to fit branding, a full narrative and CTA into every five-second opening. Published lift claims are not predictions for this project.

### 4. Write toward a specific motivation

[GameRefinery, How motivations are formed in GameRefinery?, 2023-02-13](https://docs.gamerefinery.com/en/articles/6984461-how-motivations-are-formed-in-gamerefinery)

The vendor describes a survey/model-based framework with twelve motivational drivers and eight archetypes, while acknowledging the limits of any taxonomy. Project interpretation: distinguish desires such as improving skills, solving and expression before choosing a premise. The framework is not evidence that a particular game's audience has a given motivation; that still requires game/audience evidence. No vendor access or purchase is needed for this skill.

## Proposed application and validation

Start with the viewer's desire and a specific anticipated event; develop meaningful progression and a truthful connection to the next game segment. Compare plain-text premises before visual execution. This synthesis is inferred, supported by converging recommendations and the owner's HOOK-001 rejection, not by measured audience response.

Use the local reference collection to examine different mechanisms, including sensory action and challenge. Calibrate story judgment through owner feedback and preserve rejected versions. Later campaign tests must assess the intended audience response and downstream results; an attractive render or high internal score does not validate engagement.
