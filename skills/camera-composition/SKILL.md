# Camera and Composition

## Purpose

Define how the viewer sees the game and how the camera guides attention through a mobile ad. Preserve gameplay clarity and safe framing across placements.

## Inputs

- Approved creative brief
- Visual direction
- Gameplay footage or source assets
- Target format, placement, and aspect ratio
- Platform safe-area requirements

## Camera decisions

Choose intentionally:

- Top-down, isometric, side, first-person, or third-person view
- Camera height and perspective
- Wide, medium, close-up, macro, or extreme close-up
- Field of view and distortion
- Gameplay board visibility
- Subject scale and focal point
- Background depth and clutter

Use the closest view that preserves the viewer’s ability to understand the action.

## Composition

Define:

- Primary focal point
- Secondary focal point
- Subject position
- Rule of thirds or center framing
- Negative space for captions
- Safe zones for UI and CTA
- Direction of player movement
- Visual hierarchy through scale, contrast, and placement

Do not place critical gameplay, text, or CTA elements near crop boundaries.

## Camera movement

Use movement only when it improves attention, understanding, emotion, or reward:

- Push-in and pull-out
- Pan and tilt
- Tracking
- Orbit
- Parallax
- Whip movement
- Camera punch
- Controlled shake

Avoid movement that makes gameplay unreadable or feels disconnected from the input.

## Zoom, pan, and cutting rules

- Zoom toward the important action before the key move.
- Pull out when progress or board state needs to be understood.
- Pan with the subject only when the movement is necessary.
- Cut on action, impact, reveal, or clear emotional change.
- Preserve screen direction and spatial continuity.
- Use a stable shot when the mechanic requires concentration.
- Use close-ups for input and wide shots for progress or context.

## Reframing

When adapting a master asset to another placement:

1. Preserve the focal action.
2. Preserve the player input and consequence.
3. Keep critical text and CTA inside the safe area.
4. Recompose rather than simply crop when necessary.
5. Check that the new frame does not imply a different gameplay action.

## Review rules

- Review every intended aspect ratio.
- Confirm the core action remains visible after cropping.
- Confirm camera movement does not hide or falsify gameplay.
- Reject unnecessary motion, unstable framing, and unreadable UI.
- Record assumptions when source footage cannot support the requested camera move.

## Boundaries

This skill does not define the campaign hypothesis, color system, export encoding, or detailed particle and sound design.
