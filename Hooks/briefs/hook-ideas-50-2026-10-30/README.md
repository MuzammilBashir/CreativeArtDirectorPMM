## Scope and assumptions

All concepts propose original fictional scenarios or mechanics for exploration. No target game, audience research, placement or Game DNA has been supplied. Each connection names what a real game's footage or validated prototype would need to demonstrate. None certifies a commercial-game mechanic, puzzle solvability, product benefit, or audience response. Character concepts are original story proposals, not additions to a locked style-restyle assignment.

Each story targets five seconds in 9:16 portrait, with a first-frame prompt and an image-to-video prompt. Use the two prompts in sequence: create and review the opening still, then use that approved still as the motion reference. Prompts describe intent, not a guarantee of exact model timing or geometry. Sound directions are proposed synchronized cues; if unsupported by the selected model, add them in Mixer rather than claiming generated audio.

These are written plans only. No images or videos were generated, no tools/models were selected, and no paid runs were made. At production time follow the existing rules: lowest-cost capable available model, smallest supported image drafts, native 480p video drafts, reviewed 720p final, and the authorized attempt limits. Check actual model capabilities and prices then. No Higgsfield preset or slash-command workflow is implied by these plain-language prompts.

## Self-review method

Scores are subjective assessments of the **written story and visual plan**, not reviews of actual assets or predicted engagement. The user explicitly requested self-scoring. This study rubric does not replace the project's production approval rules or establish a new standing numerical threshold.

| Dimension, 0–10 | What Art Director assesses |
| --- | --- |
| Care | A specific player desire and locally meaningful stakes; emotional purpose rather than decoration |
| Anticipation | A concrete outcome, question or event worth waiting for; an understandable opening |
| Causality | An action changes the situation, or a reveal changes its meaning; more than chronological activity |
| Payoff/bridge | A purposeful resolution or open question, and a plausible connection to the proposed game appeal |
| Composition | Focal hierarchy, view, crop margins and stable spatial direction suited to a portrait phone preview |
| Visual language/readability | Coherent style, value/color roles, simple materials and uncluttered mobile-scale intent |
| Motion/audio | Readable action, anticipation/contact/consequence, justified camera/effects and synchronized sound intent |
| Feasibility | Readable in five seconds; manageable continuity, object interactions and generation complexity |

Story = (Care + Anticipation + Causality + Payoff/bridge) × 2.5. Visual plan = (Composition + Visual language/readability + Motion/audio + Feasibility) × 2.5. Overall = mean of these two scores. Game DNA = N/A for all 50.

Anchors: 5–6 = substantial weakness; 7 = usable core with significant revision risk; 8 = clear promising plan with a named limitation; 9 = particularly strong written candidate with limited identifiable weaknesses; 10 is not evidence of perfection and should be exceptional. Ratings compare these proposals within this study, not against campaign results. A high overall number must not conceal a weak story or missing factual support.

Each idea records a strength, weakness, and revision already applied. Even a high score is not production approval. Actual imagery, phone-size readability, movement, sound, style match and game truth remain unassessed until the relevant evidence exists.
