# A Moment That's Mine

Concept ID: HOOK-001. Version: 1. Date: 2026-10-29.
Status: story and visual-direction draft, ready for first-frame exploration. No generated asset is approved.

## Purpose and feeling

Give the viewer a recognizable moment of choosing a small break, then make them curious about what the character chooses to do next. Intended emotional progression: **recognition -> permission -> anticipation**.

The reason to care is simple: sometimes every minute seems to belong to somebody else. The character takes one small moment back. This is an emotional hypothesis, not a claim about audience response or a game's health benefits.

**Story in one sentence:** A tired desk worker deliberately closes a laptop, lets out a tiny breath, and picks up a phone as a faint smile returns: “A moment that's mine.”

## Brief interpretation and assumptions

- Use the work/leisure territory in the reviewed `Hook_NoMeTime` reference and the collection study brief as the starting mechanism. Invent a different story: no golf equipment, indoor swinging, meeting deception, earnings claim, or copied character.
- This is a phase-one creative exercise. No game, audience research, platform or Game DNA was supplied. Audience hypothesis: adults who recognize competing demands on their time.
- The user explicitly authorizes Art Director to write the story and visual directions. The study brief is not evidence of a real game's features; this production direction adds clearly labeled creative assumptions.
- Format assumption: 9:16 portrait. Target duration: **4.5 seconds**. Draft stills use the selected model's smallest supported resolution; video drafts remain 480p and final video 720p. Check model-supported dimensions before generation.
- Emotional payoff is the character's choice to pause, not a promise that a product treats stress. No game UI, rewards, mechanics or branded hardware are generated.

## Visual directions for Higgsfield

**Style:** clean, gamey semi-realistic 3D, feasible as a mobile-engine scene. Realistic adult proportions, simplified skin and cloth shading, moderate geometry, baked/simple lighting and soft simple shadows. This follows the shared visual feedback; it is not claimed to match an unspecified game.

**Character:** one original fictional adult in their early thirties, short dark wavy hair, muted teal long-sleeve crewneck. Express fatigue through posture and eyes, not caricatured despair. No resemblance request, mascot, jewelry, logos or elaborate wardrobe details.

**Environment:** compact home desk, quiet warm-gray wall, simplified shelf with two abstract book shapes well behind the subject. Only laptop, mug, paper stack and phone on the desk. No legible writing or fake app screens. Set dressing cannot be more detailed than the face and hands.

**Color roles:** muted teal shirt for character identity; warm ivory skin highlights and desk accents; slate-gray laptop; warm-gray background; muted coral mug as a secondary accent. These are proposed palette roles, not sampled reference colors. Keep saturation restrained and highlights soft.

**Lighting:** a broad soft light from camera-left and gentle ambient fill. Stable exposure and hue throughout. No dramatic lighting change when the phone is picked up. No cinematic grade, lens flare, volumetric light, film grain, heavy motion blur or depth-of-field blur.

**Camera:** 9:16, eye-level medium close-up from slightly left of the desk center. Face in the upper-middle, laptop lid and hands in the center/lower-middle. Use a single continuous setup; at most a gentle final push. Focal hierarchy: expression -> closing hand/lid -> phone. Keep face, working hand and phone inside the central 80% width and middle 75% height as an internal crop allowance, not a platform-certified safe area.

**Motion:** believable lid hinge, restrained shoulder release and hand reach; no sudden smile snap or morphing fingers. The reach should follow the decision/exhale rather than racing with the closing action. Use contrast between a held decision, clear close, and small settled breath. Preserve hand identity and which hand performs each action across frames.

**Text:** no text inside generated mocks. Optional caption “A moment that's mine.” can be typeset later after placement is known. The story must still read with sound off and caption absent.

## Mixer intention

Cut on the poised-thumb action into supplied, verified gameplay that begins with a clear input. Exact game, next shot, motion match, audio overlap and placement are **unknown**. The current ending is a bridge candidate, not a certified match to a destination asset. Before delivery, confirm the chosen game actually supports the intended tone and transition.

## Risks and evaluation

- If the first frame reads as generic office stock rather than a person making a choice, strengthen eye line/posture and simplify the environment.
- If the smile appears before the break is chosen, restore the emotional order.
- If the phone looks like work rather than personal choice, use the settled posture, warmer expression and optional line; do not add invented game UI.
- If 4.5 seconds feels rushed, simplify the reach and camera motion; do not speed up every beat.
- If the destination game clashes with the quiet anticipation, revise the bridge or choose another concept when Game DNA arrives.
